What a television client should own

November 10, 2026

Note by Armel Tenkiang.

On this page
  1. Two responsibilities
  2. Offline does not mean everything is permitted
  3. The verification boundary
  4. Scope of this note

A cache makes a disconnected device useful. It does not give that device authority over all system data.

Two responsibilities

In Home, the local hub retains authoritative records. The television client presents a cached projection and keeps actions awaiting acknowledgement. Separating those responsibilities avoids confusing displayed state with state accepted by the system.

Offline does not mean everything is permitted

A client can continue showing what it knows without pretending every operation is available. Permitted actions enter a queue; the outcome comes from the hub. The expected revision makes the state behind a change explicit.

If the record has changed, the conflict needs to return to the reader. Interface continuity does not justify a silent overwrite.

The verification boundary

The reviewed implementation includes a native Android TV interface, a persistent queue and a paired channel. Recorded test/build results concern that code: they do not replace acceptance on a physical television with real network conditions and input.

Scope of this note

This note describes the 27 October 2026 Home source snapshot and previously recorded test/build evidence. It does not claim physical-TV acceptance or public release.
